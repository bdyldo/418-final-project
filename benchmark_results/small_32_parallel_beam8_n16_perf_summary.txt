Main conclusions
----------------
1. The dominant bottleneck in this run is OpenMP synchronization/waiting
   overhead, not a single serial merge or collision-detection function.

2. The main useful compute bottleneck is A* search, especially:
   - unordered_map / unordered_set lookups
   - memory allocation and free traffic
   - repeated small sorting in orderedMoves()

3. Collision detection and merge validation are not the primary hotspots in
   this profile. They appear in the report, but at much lower overhead than
   A* and libgomp waiting.

4. The plateau in scaling for the beam-locked small_32 run is therefore best
   explained by:
   - too little useful parallel work per repair iteration
   - substantial waiting/synchronization overhead in libgomp
   - expensive A* internals once a worker actually gets useful work


Short report-ready takeaway
---------------------------
Profiling the beam-locked small_32 parallel planner at N=16 shows that the
program does not spend most of its time in a serial merge step. Instead, more
than half of sampled cycles occur inside libgomp synchronization/spin loops,
while the largest application hotspot is A* search and its hash-table/memory
management overhead. perf stat reports only 0.935 CPUs utilized on average,
indicating that the thread team is mostly underutilized because each repair
iteration exposes too little independent work.
